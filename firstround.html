Your First Round

Playing your first round of golf can be intimidating, but knowing what to expect makes it much easier. You do not need to play perfectly or know every rule. Focus on keeping up with the group, being respectful of other golfers, and enjoying your time on the course.

Before Your Tee Time

Try to arrive at the course about 30 minutes before your tee time. This gives you time to check in, get your clubs ready, and hit a few practice putts. Being early also keeps you from feeling rushed when it is time to head to the first tee.

At the First Tee

Be ready when it is your turn to play. Make sure the group ahead of you is safely out of range before hitting. Do not worry if you are nervous or hit a bad first shot. Every golfer has been a beginner at some point.

Keep Up With the Group

You do not have to play fast, but you should try to keep up with the group in front of you. Think about your next shot while other players are hitting and have your club ready when it is your turn. If you are having trouble on a hole, it is okay to pick up your ball and move on.

Take Care of the Course

Replace divots when possible, repair ball marks on the green, and rake bunkers after hitting from them. You should also avoid driving a golf cart into areas where carts are not allowed. Taking care of the course is an important part of golf etiquette.
