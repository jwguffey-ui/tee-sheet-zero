Golf Basics

You do not need to know every rule in golf before playing your first round. Knowing the basic rules, scoring, and etiquette will make it much easier to understand what is happening on the course. Most of the smaller rules will make more sense as you continue playing.

How Golf Is Scored

The goal of golf is to complete each hole using as few strokes as possible. Every time you hit the ball it counts as one stroke. At the end of the round your strokes from each hole are added together to give you your total score.

Understanding Par

Each hole has a par which represents the number of strokes an experienced golfer is expected to need. Most holes are a par 3, par 4, or par 5. For example, finishing a par 4 in four strokes means you made par. Finishing it in three strokes is a birdie and finishing it in five strokes is a bogey.

Basic Golf Terms

The tee box is where each hole begins. The fairway is the shorter grass that leads toward the green. The green is the area surrounding the hole where you use your putter. Bunkers are areas filled with sand, while the rough is the longer grass found around the fairway and green.

Golf Etiquette

Golf etiquette is mostly about being respectful of the course and the people around you. Stay quiet while another golfer is hitting, avoid walking through another player's putting line, repair ball marks when possible, and keep up with the group ahead of you. If your group is falling behind, be ready to hit when it is your turn.

Keep Learning

You will learn more rules as you play, so do not feel like you need to memorize everything before your first round. Understanding scoring, basic course terms, and good etiquette is enough to get started.
